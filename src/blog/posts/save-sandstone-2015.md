The Save Sandstone Climbing Festival started in 2013 to raise money to purchase land that has beautiful sandstone boulders, perfect for climbing. The area has over 40 boulder problems according to mountain project at the time of this writing. The MCA was able to raise enough money with the help of the Save Sandstone Festival to purchase the land, known as Eldon’s. Named after the original property owner that allowed climbers to climb on the land. Why are we still having the festival if the money was raised and the land now belongs to the climbers? Well, because it’s fun!




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*ApujzGM0ofddSAbb.jpg)



Three of my friends all met up at my place around 9am on Saturday, filled up two cars with our gear and my dog (Striker) then got on the road. We arrived at Robinson Park in Sandstone MN about an hour and a half later. We first set off to find a camping spot which was easily acquired off the main trail where we parked our cars. We set up our hammocks, then grab our gear to do some bouldering across the river where Eldon’s is. We pray that we will find something dry. It rained the night before and climbing on wet sandstone is bad, as it can break the holds ruining the problem for other boulders. Even though we knew the climbing part of the trip might be a bust set out anyways because camping, camaraderie and taco night still sounded like a great weekend.




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*tpY7ithU07SsB52b.jpg)



When we got across the bridge and into Eldon’s we ran into more climbers. We set out to the warm up boulder. It’s dry! Miraculously the rain dried up in in the morning sun! We end up with 8 people and a dog at the warm up boulder. We do some lines to get warmed up. We have so many pads laid out on the ground it’s like being at the gym. After having some fun at the warm up boulder we head over to The River Boulder.

The River Boulder is a beautiful boulder that’s obviously next to the river.The Kettle River to be exact. Depending on the day you might need a few pads stacked up to reach the start on the boulder. This boulder is home of “The Best v5 in Minnesota” among some other great lines. More and more people started showing up, I lost count. It’s great to so many people hanging out, having a good time, and climbing in the sun.




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*wPqox7poKR4XfvG7.jpg)



Our crew grows tired, the all you can eat tacos and cold beer are calling our name. So we head back across the bridge to Robinson Park. The tacos and beer cost $10, which is a steal. The money goes to the MCA, a noble cause for us climbers. We eat some tacos, hang out with some great folks and drink a few beers. After dark movies are played up on the side of the cliff, appropriately named the stage wall. Lots of different climbing movies are shown, none of the names I can remember, damn my goldfish memory. I grow tired of just sitting and decide to go for a pre-bed climb with a friend. I don’t normally climb ropes, but apparently after a beer or two I’m easily convinced. I pick out a simple looking 5.9 called Morphine. My friend goes first and sends it really quickly, I follow after him taking my time, but I do it! My first time night climbing (sport climbing I might add)! Happy, proud, and exhausted from a full day of climbing, I head to my hammock and go to bed.

The morning is really wet. It almost feels like rain, except it’s not. It’s all the dew dripping off the leaves, a few finding me right in the middle of the forehead. There is a fog coming off the river that’s really beautiful. It’s early not sure how early but the sun isn’t up. Josh and I start to take down our hammocks and packing up the car. We are skeptical about climbing with how wet everything is. We watch the sunrise up over the tree line across the kettle river over the river boulder. We head back to our camp spot in the woods, and convince others to go to Amy’s Country Cafe. We eat a huge breakfast, and drink lots of coffee. Josh and I decide to head back to Minneapolis while a few others go back to do some rope climbing. What a great trip, see you at Save Sandstone 2016!_Originally published at_ [_adventure.andrewreitz.com_](http://adventure.andrewreitz.com/post/131719593719/savesandstone2015)_._
