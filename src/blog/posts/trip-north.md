The weekend after Thanksgiving my roommate Paul and I decided to go bikepacking up north on the C.J. Ramstad/North Shore State Trail. After a quick run to our favorite bike shop. The Alt on Lake and Lyndale, we started making our way up to Duluth sometime in the late afternoon. The trail is a state trail generally used for snowmobiling, and sort of follows the Superior Hiking Trail. We probably rolled in around 1:30pm, with the intent of doing about 50 miles to a camping spot on the trail. The weather was as perfect as it could be for almost being December. High of 33, a low of 18, just a small dusting of snow, and most importantly no wind.




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*U9iEwER4WeUvAUeq.jpg)



We set off down the trail and start to wonder if we are going to make it the 50 miles. It’s a marsh that has frozen over. A mix of ice, bumpy frozen ground and cattails. We trudge through, cross a road leading us to a more reasonable path. It’s still frozen ground but now it’s on grass, making the ride considerably less bumping. Heading up a hill, we shift into low gears (of course), there is a sound like a stick snapping and Paul stops. He looks at his bike and starts to laugh. He derailleur is bent backwards and stuck in the spokes of his wheel. We stare at it for a bit, trying to come up with a plan.




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*q36shtrOO2Hk4jSs.jpg)



I pull out a flask, take a swig and pass it to Paul. You need to drink hard liquor before it gets below freezing and causes internal frostbite (or better solution don’t drink while it’s really cold out, I forgot about that rule). We come up with a plan. We walk back to the car, head to a bike shop and see if we can’t get it fixed. Our second bike shop in a day.

The diagnostics are in about Paul’s bike. The limit screw was stripped, so in low gear the derailleur could go down into the spokes. The derailer is slightly bent, the hanger broken. The shop Ski Hut, has a new hanger and replace that, but not another screw or derailleur. Paul’s bike is usable but not in the bottom two gears. Else the derailleur will end up in the same shape.

With this news we head to dinner at one of my favorite restaurants in Duluth, Burrito Union, to drink some Brew House Beer, eat some delicious burritos and form a new plan. The plan we come up with, since it is becoming dark out, is to attempt to go back where we were and try to make it to the first shelter, about 6 miles in. This seems pretty reasonable in the dark and dropping temperatures.

Back out on the trail we run into giant icy patches, large never ending hills, rocky bumpy sections and crazy fast icy downhills, all at night. All and all it was a blast, as long as you hold a good line you shouldn’t burn out too quickly due to all your power being absorbed by the soft ground. If it had been any warmer or any snowier we would have been on the wrong bikes (we were riding our cross bikes). We make it to the campsite in what seems a reasonable amount of time and find that there is a shelter there all ready for us to use. Instead of setting up the tent we just throw down a tarp, our air mattresses and sleeping bags. We both stayed fairly warm throughout the night, but the morning was brutal. Nothing like putting on frozen boots.




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*ih0pVVY6eBNmrnp0.jpg)



We pack as quickly as we can and set off, stopping a few times to warm our hands and feet. It’s a very strange feeling to be sweating but not be able to feel your toes. Looking at our tracks the night before we see that we had some great lines. The trail looks way icier than I had thought at night, leaving me impressed that we made it as far as we did in the darkness. We make it back to the car our feet still code, and begin packing up. We go to Uncle Louis a must visit breakfast joint in Duluth to finish off the trip, thinking to ourselves how lucky we were that that derailleur didn’t break further in on our trip. I plan to head back up to the train another time to try and make it lot further in.
