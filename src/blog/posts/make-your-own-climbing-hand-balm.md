There are all sorts of climbing balms for sale but they are all pretty much the same ingredients. So, instead of paying $15 for a little tin, why not make your own? I’ve included links to products I’ve used before, but by all means use whichever you prefer.

The first step is to get a medium to large glass jar that can fit inside of one of your pots. This jar is where you would do all the melting and mixing. You don’t want to clean out a mess of wax and oil from your pots, instead use this glass jar. Place it in a pot of water on the stove. The water should get hot, but not to a rolling boil. If it does start to boil just turn the heat down. Instead of cleaning up the jar when you are finished just save it to use it again.

**Oils**

There are a few different oils you can pick and choose from. I prefer jojoba and almond oil because almond oil has vitamins E, A, and B. Jojoba is antibacterial, has vitamin E, and is very similar to oils that our body produces.

There are other oils that you can add or replace with in this recipe. Some suggestions are as follows.

Sesame Oil — Contains Vitamin E, and also has anti-fungal properties.

Grape Seed Oil — has vitamin E.

Argan Oil — Contains vitamin A and vitamin E, antioxidants, Omega-6 fatty acids, and linoleic acid

**Essential Oils**

I prefer tea tree and lavender. Tea tree oil is known for its antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties. Two things I want in a climbing balm. Lavender oil is known for its calming and relaxing qualities, soothing skin irritations, helping skin recover quickly, and eases muscle tension.

**Instructions**

Now that you have all your ingredients and a double boiler setup with your jar. Add all the ingredients to the jar. I suggest adding the beeswax, coconut oil, and shea butter first to allow it to melt before adding the other oils. Once all the ingredients are liquefied stir up the mixture and then remove from heat. Slowly pour the liquid into your tin or jar and let cool. For quicker cooling put in the freezer.

My version of the balm is pretty solid, because I dislike my hands feeling greasy. This makes it a bit hard to get out of the tins, so just use the back of your fingernail to get small chunks out of the tin, then let your fingers heat it up to be able to easily apply. If you want a more oily balm going down to just a half cup of beeswax should do the trick.

This recipe makes quite a bit of balm so share with your friends, and don’t just use it for your hands try it as a lip balm too.
