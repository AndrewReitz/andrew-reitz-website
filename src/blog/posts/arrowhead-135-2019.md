This is an overview of my ride, what I brought with me, and what I did for training. This is not a guide and you shouldn’t copy what I did. You should instead do lots of research and practice a lot. I’ll do an overview of things and if you want more details feel free to ask in the comments but there is far too much to write if I wanted to include all the nitty gritty here. I didn’t finish the race, it was one of the coldest years they have seen, and I’m really disappointed that I didn’t finish, but that’s okay it happens. this just drives me to train harder and finish next year.

The race is a 135-mile race on the Arrowhead State Trail in Minnesota, starting in International Falls (known as the icebox of the country) and ending in Tower Minnesota. It’s the last Monday — Wednesday of January which is historically the coldest time of year in Minnesota. It’s on weekdays because it’s a snowmobile trail, and there will be fewer snowmobilers on weekdays. You may bike, run, or ski the race, and there is a decent amount of required gear that you have to take with you to ensure your safety in the extremely cold conditions. If you want to know more about the race or required gear check out the [Arrowhead’s Website](http://arrowheadultra.com/).




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/600/1*t_-kLv8gMdlObZ8YFGBHJg.jpeg)

All my gear laid out before packing the car. Don’t want to forget anything!



There is a mandatory gear check before the race. My partner and I arrived the Sunday before the race to do the gear check, settle in, and go to the mandatory pre-race meeting. This is the usual race meeting where rules are gone over. There is also a raffle and a spaghetti dinner. I should mention that Sunday international falls it was -44℉ that Sunday morning. After the meeting, we go back to the hotel where Kristi plans out places to possibly spectate and I repack my gear, ensuring I have everything and that it’s packed how I want it. The race is about a mile and a half away from the hotel so I plan to drive over there which means I won’t have all my gear on my bike in the morning. I check the weather and it is supposed to be -20℉ at the 7 am start. I get as many things onto the bike as I can, and lay out my clothes, put water in the kettle to boil for hot water in my water bottles in the morning and go to bed. I’m really not excited about putting my frame bag on when it’s that cold.




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/600/1*pwnBU61HQSXYmwvV83UWSw.jpeg)

As ready as I can be in the hotel room.



Fast forwards through some wake ups and checking my clock through the night and it’s time to start getting ready. I boil water to fill up my water bottles, get dressed, get my bike on the car and we are off. When we pull in I move quickly to get the rest of my bags on the bike, take off the bike rack and get my gear on. I won a ColdAvenger at the raffle the night before and thought -10℉ what a good idea to try something out that I hadn’t used in practice. Yeah it was only -10℉ instead of -20℉ like my weather app said, never thought I would be excited for -10℉! I had a similar issue with clothing, it hadn’t gotten to -10℉ this year in Minneapolis so I decided to add a layer.  
I check in, turn on my lights on and we are off! The race starts with fireworks going off, and a mass roll out. Because I’m fresh and have so many layers on I start sweating, which is not what you want. Also in the chaos of starting I have my sunglasses on top of my head, they froze up from my sweaty head and never really become usable. At about 10 miles into the race I had to stop and take off the ColdAvenger, it’s pretty warm but has no way to eat or drink without stopping which is not ideal. I also take a layer off. There really isn’t much to report after this. I make it to the first checkpoint, it’s pretty flat and uneventful. From the first checkpoint to the second it’s much more hilly which made it more interesting, and it was snowing which hurt my eyes. I tried to wear goggles but they iced up very quickly. Goggles checked out fine in training, but I think it was windier those days.




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/600/1*AnOZZvahXDJu80SFv7b96g.jpeg)

Warm and happy at the 1st checkpoint



I grind on in for which feels like forever (3 or so hours I didn’t see anyone else) and finally make it to MelGeorge’s the second checkpoint. I know it would be slow rolling, but it was starting to get to me. I check in and I decide to spend the night which was kind of my plan originally, to take it one day at a time and try to avoid biking at night when it’s even colder. I wake up in the morning and see that the temp is -23℉, with a high of -18℉. I decide to get breakfast at the restaurant and will decide what I’m going to do after. Note: you’re allowed to utilize any of the facilities at the checkpoints. The rule is no outside help, which roughly translates don’t receive anything other people can’t get, to keep the race fair.   
   
I eat breakfast, have a few moments where I think “I can do this”, then realize that it’s crazy cold and I don’t want to have any issues while it’s crazy cold and I’m 30 miles from anyone or anything. Also, my legs are still pretty stiff, and sleeping didn’t seem to help, I didn’t go on enough long rides with the boots I choose and found out the hard way that they hurt my ankles. At around 9 am I decided to throw in the towel.### The Gear

### Food




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/600/1*IFzl0fFc_YsXSx41SQoilg.jpeg)

All the junk food!



I’m not going to list out what I brought because mostly I don’t remember. I brought so much stuff. I will say you have to finish the race with 3k calories, and for that, I brought a big jar of peanut butter. The food I ate most on the trail were Gardetto’s (bag in one pocket), Watermelon Sour Patch Kids (bag in the other pocket), Paydays, and Clif Bloks. I think between my drop bag and what I had on me it was around 10k calories. Yeah, I eat lots of junk food while on these kinds of races, but that’s one of the reasons to do these races 😃. I would love to eat more “real” food, but it’s hard finding things that stay mostly edible when it’s in a freezer.### Training

I got asked by a few people what I did for training so figured I’d throw in a section about it. Basically, I biked a lot. I did a large number of gravel races, one overnight bikepacking race, and a few others that I thought would be good training such as Tuscobia 80 which is as close to Arrowhead conditions as I could find. I tried to do a few practices runs, although the weather in Minneapolis didn’t want to cooperate, and did most of my riding with all of my gear to make sure I knew everything inside and out. I also did some winter camping since finding out your sleeping bag isn’t warm enough or your pad doesn’t cut it when you need it most is bad!
