The Almanzo 100 is known as the “Granddaddy of all gravel races”, and is a 100-mile bike ride in southern Minnesota, starting out of Spring Valley. The course is almost all gravel roads with a few paved connectors and a single minimum maintenance road.




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*rVe9qCjbpMX6mNtQ.jpg)



_Freshly laminated cue cards_

Where to start, I guess I decided to do it because I wanted to see if I could. I also really enjoy biking, so there is that too! I have heard the Almanzo talked about by many and it seemed like a good goal to have. Many people referred to it as a “suffer fest”, sounds like my cup of tea. I had missed my 100-mile gravel ride (what would have also been my first century) I planned on doing this summer with a good buddy of mine due to food poisoning so why not replace that with the Almanzo? I hadn’t ever biked more than 50 miles before. but I’m stubborn, and enough people told me I couldn’t.

Training, I didn’t really train too much, I just bike a lot on mountain bikes and on gravel trails when the mountain bike trails were closed. If I didn’t have enough time to get in a long bike ride, I would go for a run, and on my “rest days” I would go rock climbing. Sometimes biking to the climbing gym to do so (rest days are hard). The few days I really threw down on some training would be the Tuesday and Wednesday about 2 weeks before the race. I set a timer on my phone for 2 hours and biked as far west on the Luce Line Trail until the timer went off. When it did I turned around and would bike home. I even found some gravel roads while I was out that way, which I explored a little bit. These days were cold, windy, and wet. Wednesday I bailed early as I couldn’t feel my feet after about 10 minutes into my ride, it was pouring rain the whole time.




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*Go8i4-IOj5LWi45e.jpg)



_Wet cold training ride_

The next big training day came a week before the Almanzo. It was mothers day weekend, which moved the time with my son, opening up the weekend. I took this opportunity to head to the Mammoth Gravel Classic course that I had missed out doing a few weeks before due to aforementioned sickness. I went out by myself with what I thought was enough water and food to keep going the entire day. This would be my first century. I didn’t really have any choices for failure, I had to make it unless there was some sort of catastrophe, I wasn’t about to beg a local for a ride to my car.

It was a beautiful sunny day, and very windy. I could barely hear my music over my headphones due to the howling wind. This ride taught me a great deal about my gear, and definitely taught me to always bring cash while on a bike ride (I ended up at a bar that didn’t take credit cards, it took a while to figure out how to fix this situation). The sand on this ride was brutal, but luckily it was only in the first 30 or 40 so miles. Long story short my first century down and I was feeling pretty good. I was very happy with myself for completing it. I think even with my gear adjustments, pee breaks, and stop at a bar (sickness of kind bars is what brought me to the bar) I managed to do it in something like 10.5 hours. No great, but not bad considering I needed to figure out how to get cash at a bar during this.

Fast forward a week to Friday before the Almanzo. I’ve got everything packed, better food options than just bars and gu. I am supposed to be going with a friend of mine but haven’t heard when and where we are meeting in the morning. I text him as I’m about to go to bed and he says he’s bailing. It’s supposed to be cold, and windy, and he heard a lot of the gravel is washed out, I say it’s Minnesota what do you expect. I decided to go it alone even though I know that bailing would be an easier option.

I get terrible sleep. I toss and turn, and I have my alarm set to 4:30 am. I tried to get 6 hours of sleep which is a pretty standard night’s sleep for me. I think I am lucky if I got anywhere near 4 due to nerves and doubt. I wake up to my alarm, get dressed, take the dog out, hope in my car and drive. I drive with no music, my Bluetooth in my car was acting up, and the silence is kind of nice. Due to my nerves and poor choices of drinking lots of electrolyte water as well as smoothies for breakfast, I have to stop and pee twice. I think I peed another 4 times before the race starts.




![image](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*7QcfbOL8q8x8YJjF.jpg)



_Just before the race all set to go_

To skip ahead a bit here, I register for the race get my bike ready and then sit in my car trying to stay warm and keep my nerves down for about an hour and a half until the race starts. I follow the others down the road to Main Street of Spring Valley. The race starts and away we go. I decided I should take a steady pace and not try to go fast. This was probably a good choice, and I stick to the right side of the road as many others pass me. About 3 miles in, on the second gravel road the wind hits, it is brutal and is going to be with us most of the ride. There a quite a few towns folk that have come to cheer us on. It puts a big smile on my face and I just keep pedaling.

I make it to the first stop, I waited to pee until I get here only to find there are no bathrooms, which is fine with me, just was unexpected. This stop is where I realize that “self-supported” has a different definition and explains why I saw many people with only 2 water bottles and no bags. When I saw these people I thought to myself they are going to die. But now I see, self-supported means you can bring your own crew. Oh well, I wasn’t going to win this race anyways, but I am hauling a lot of weight.

I down a pb+j tortilla (more compact that a sandwich), a bunch of banana chips, a Lara bar, and a Gu. I’ve been trying to maintain a Gu every 45 minutes but with how bad the wind is it’s pretty hard to take my hand off my handle bar fish one out and down it. So instead, I’ve been eating them when ever I can. I stretch my legs for a second and hop back on my bike. The road is the first paved road I think in 40 miles, it’s still pretty bumpy though.

Nothing eventful to write about here, I make it to the next stop in Forestville state park. I eat a bunch more food and get back on my bike, I realize there is not much reason for me to stop at these rest stops and I can eat and pee pretty much anywhere, I guess they just seem like logical places to stop, but I skip the 3rd one. I think somewhere between the 2nd and 3rd stops there was the minimum maintenance road, in this section there could have been a water crossing, but despite the water of the week before it was bone dry and I just fly right over the larger than normal rocks in the road.

The 3rd rest area somewhere around my 70 has a sign as you return to gravel that says “Riding Gravel”, similar to other yellow caution signs you might see on the road. Not sure if this was for drivers to know they were getting back on gravel or just for us cyclists but for some reason this put a big smile on my face (probably lack of sleep and exhaustion).

Mile 90, the big hill, this is the only hill I walked up the whole time, it was huge. As soon as I saw it I hopped off my bike and walked up it. A nice lady who I’d been around (she passes me, I pass here that whole leap frog game) says the hill is too steep for her Garmen to even tell her what grade it is, fun times. After the hill it sort of all blends together, more gravel, than a turn onto a path, under a bridge (am I getting to the end), then there it is the finish line. I pass it get some cheers and have a big smile on my face.

I was tempted (and maybe tried but didn’t have cell service) to post on facebook first one to the finish line with a beer for me gets $100 while walking up the big hill (another side note, after being on a bike mostly by yourself this long you start thinking crazy things, like in Dorry’s voice just keep biking, just keep biking). Luckily I had picked a good spot to park, my car is right on the other side of the park from the finish line. As I head to the car and drive home I meet a group of people from Iowa, and they give me a beer! Woo! I drink a beer and talk with them for a bit before throwing my bike in the car, and heading home. The first thing I do when in the car is called my mom of course.

_Happy to be home after ~4 hours on the road, and ~9 hours in the saddle_

For those of you wondering, here is a quick list of my gear. I wore some fox bike shorts, some moisture-wicking running shirt, Swrve softshell hoodie and pants, wool socks, 5.10 all mountain shoes, and a buff to keep the dust out of my mouth and noise. My bike is a Kona Jake the Snake, with some Teravail gravel tires. I had a 3 liter hydro flask in a small backpack, two 1.5 liters bottles in cages, and banjo brothers seat bag with two tubes, super glue, and some small zip ties, a Revelate Designs frame bag holding my food (way too much to list out) a pump a tool and some TP just in case, a Revelate Designs gas tank with my phone, backup battery, larger zip ties, and Gu. I also had just cue cards and a cheap cyclo computer to get distance to go off of (I can go into more detail if anyone is interested but it’s late and I’m tired right now).

Oh, and lastly, I did it in 9 hours and 22 minutes, I guess not bad for my second century, I mean I’m really just happy I finished._Originally published at_ [_adventure.andrewreitz.com_](http://adventure.andrewreitz.com/post/145434220574/almanzo-100-2016)_._
